With Content Independence Day 2026, [Enterprise Bot Management](/bots/get-started/bot-management/) customers get two new tools that make bot traffic easier to understand: [BotBase](/bots/botbase/), a searchable directory of every bot Cloudflare tracks, and [Business Insights](/bots/business-insights/), a dashboard that shows how much value each crawler sends back to your business.

BotBase is Cloudflare's directory of all known bots and agents, available directly in the dashboard. It shows how Cloudflare classifies each bot by behavior — Search, Agent, Training, and other categories such as Transact, Data Collection, SEO, and Ads Verification — so you can understand why a given crawler is visiting you. It also shows successful (`2xx` and `3xx`) and unsuccessful (all other status codes) request totals for the previous 24 hours. You can search and filter the full catalogue, filter your own traffic down to a single bot to investigate its activity on your zone, and copy any bot's detection ID to target it precisely in [Security rules](/security/rules/). Every tracked bot in BotBase is also published in [Cloudflare Radar's bots and agents directory](https://radar.cloudflare.com/bots/directory).

Business Insights is built for content owners and business decision-makers who want to know which bots help or harm their business, without reading rule syntax. The dashboard reports crawl-to-referral ratios both site-wide and per bot operator — comparing how often a company crawls your content against how many visitors it actually refers back — over the last 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days. Each operator is labeled with Cloudflare's [updated classification](/bots/concepts/bot/verified-bots/) and an **Outcome** of **Allowed**, **Blocked**, or **Partially blocked**. Allowed means all requests received `2xx` or `3xx` responses, Blocked means all requests received other status codes, and Partially blocked means both occurred. Partially blocked rows also show allowed and blocked request counts.

Outcomes are based on HTTP response status and do not identify the mitigation that a website owner configured for a request. Unsuccessful responses can include errors returned by the origin, such as `404` and `5xx` responses.
