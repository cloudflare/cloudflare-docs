When a custom hostname certificate is stuck in **Pending Validation** or fails to issue, a [domain control validation (DCV)](/ssl/edge-certificates/changing-dcv-method/dcv-flow/) step has not been completed successfully.

## Responsibilities

DCV for SaaS involves two parties with distinct responsibilities. Use the following table to identify who needs to act before troubleshooting a specific error.

| | SaaS provider | Your customer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Controls** | DCV method selection (TXT or HTTP), CA selection, DCV token delivery to your customer | DNS settings (NS records, DNSSEC), CAA records, DNS CNAME pointing to your SaaS target |
| **Responsible for** | Generating and sharing fresh DCV tokens, switching CA if rate limited or blocked, Workers not intercepting DCV paths | Placing DCV tokens in DNS, fixing DNSSEC, updating CAA records to allow the selected CA |

The [CA error reference](#ca-error-reference) specifies who needs to act for each error type.

:::note
DCV errors appear under `ssl.validation_errors` when you query the [Custom Hostname Details](/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/get/) endpoint.
:::

---

## Quick checklist

Before investigating a specific error, verify the following:

- [ ] **DCV tokens are placed** — your customer has added the TXT record or HTTP token that you provided to their DNS.
- [ ] **Tokens are not expired** — Let's Encrypt tokens expire after 7 days; Google Trust Services and SSL.com tokens expire after 14 days. If tokens may have expired, [refresh them](#stale-or-expired-dcv-tokens).
- [ ] **CAA records allow the CA** — CAA records are checked at every level of the DNS tree: the CA checks the custom hostname itself (for example, `shop.customer.com`) **and** the parent domain (`customer.com`). Both levels must permit Cloudflare's [partner certificate authorities](/ssl/reference/certificate-authorities/), or have no CAA records at all.
- [ ] **DNS is reachable** — your customer's authoritative DNS must respond without SERVFAIL from all geographic locations.
- [ ] **No Workers intercepting DCV paths** — if your SaaS zone uses a [Worker as the fallback origin](/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/advanced-settings/worker-as-origin/), ensure it passes through `/.well-known/pki-validation/*` and `/.well-known/acme-challenge/*` without modification.

For DCV issues that apply to all Cloudflare zones (WAF rules, redirects, DNS configuration), refer to [Troubleshooting DCV](/ssl/edge-certificates/changing-dcv-method/troubleshooting/).

## CA error reference

The following errors appear in `ssl.validation_errors` on the custom hostname object.

| Error message | Cause | Who acts | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Certificate authority encountered a SERVFAIL during DNS lookup, please check your DNS reachability.` | The CA cannot reach your customer's authoritative DNS server. | Your customer | Check DNS reachability. Ensure NS records are healthy and accessible from all geographic locations. Use [DNSViz](https://dnsviz.net/) to diagnose issues. |
| `dns problem: looking up caa for <hostname>: dnssec: bogus` | DNSSEC validation failed for the hostname. | Your customer | Fix the DNSSEC configuration at the authoritative DNS provider. Use [DNSViz](https://dnsviz.net/) to identify invalid signatures. |
| `CAA records block issuance. Please remove all CAA records or add records for this authority.` | Your customer's CAA records do not permit the selected CA to issue certificates for their domain. | Your customer | Ask your customer to add CAA records for the selected CA, or remove restrictive CAA records. Refer to [CAA records](#caa-records) for the exact records required per CA. |
| `Certificate authority encountered a multiple perspective CAA check error, please ensure your DNS is configured to allow CAA queries from all geographic perspectives.` | The CA validates from multiple geographic locations; CAA records do not return consistent results across all locations. | Your customer | Ensure CAA records have fully propagated across all authoritative DNS servers. Check for split-horizon DNS configurations that may return different results depending on query origin. |
| `The authority has rate limited these domains. Please wait for the rate limit to expire or try another authority.` | The CA has temporarily blocked certificate issuance for this domain due to too many recent requests. | You | Wait for the rate limit window to expire (Let's Encrypt: up to 7 days; Google Trust Services: varies), or switch to a different CA by calling the [Edit Custom Hostname](/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/edit/) endpoint and setting `ssl.certificate_authority` to `google`, `lets_encrypt`, or `ssl_com` (or `""` to let Cloudflare choose). |
| `Internal error with Certificate Authority. Please check later.` | Transient error on the CA side. | You | Wait and retry. If the error persists after 24 hours, switch CA using the [Edit Custom Hostname](/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/edit/) endpoint or contact [Cloudflare Support](/support/contacting-cloudflare-support/). |

## CAA records

If a custom hostname shows a `caa_error` status, your customer's CAA records are preventing the certificate authority from issuing a certificate for their domain. Your customer needs to resolve this issue.

:::caution[Check CAA records at every level]
Certificate authorities walk up the DNS tree when checking CAA records. For a custom hostname like `shop.example.com`, the CA checks CAA records at `shop.example.com` **and** at `example.com`. A restrictive CAA record on the parent domain will block issuance even if the subdomain has no CAA records.
:::

Ask your customer to check CAA records at both the subdomain and parent domain level:

```sh
dig CAA shop.example.com
dig CAA example.com
```

The records must permit the certificate authority you selected for their custom hostname. Add the appropriate records based on the selected CA:

```txt
# Google Trust Services
example.com CAA 0 issue "pki.goog; cansignhttpexchanges=yes"
example.com CAA 0 issuewild "pki.goog; cansignhttpexchanges=yes"

# Let's Encrypt
example.com CAA 0 issue "letsencrypt.org"
example.com CAA 0 issuewild "letsencrypt.org"

# SSL.com
example.com CAA 0 issue "ssl.com"
example.com CAA 0 issuewild "ssl.com"
```

If your customer does not intend to restrict certificate issuance, they can remove all CAA records. For more details, refer to [CAA records FAQ](/ssl/faq/#caa-records).

## Stale or expired DCV tokens

DCV tokens become invalid in the following situations:

- The token validity period expires before your customer places them (Let's Encrypt: 7 days; Google Trust Services and SSL.com: 14 days).
- Universal SSL is toggled off and back on for your SaaS zone.
- The custom hostname is deleted and re-created.

The custom hostname shows `ssl.status: pending_validation` and `ssl.validation_errors` reports a missing token on a domain your customer believes is correctly configured, or your customer confirms they placed the token but validation has not progressed.

To refresh the DCV tokens, send a PATCH request to the [Edit Custom Hostname](/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/edit/) endpoint. This starts a new DCV cycle and generates fresh tokens. Share the updated tokens from `ssl.validation_records` with your customer.

<Details header="Example API call">

```sh
curl --request PATCH \
  "https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/zones/{zone_id}/custom_hostnames/{custom_hostname_id}" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer <API_TOKEN>" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "ssl": {
      "method": "txt",
      "type": "dv"
    }
  }'
```

</Details>

## Timed out validation

A custom hostname enters **Timed Out Validation** (`ssl.status: timed_out`) when the DCV tokens were not placed before the retry schedule was exhausted.

To restart the DCV cycle, send a PATCH request to the [Edit Custom Hostname](/api/resources/custom_hostnames/methods/edit/) endpoint:

<Details header="Example API call">

```sh
curl --request PATCH \
  "https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/zones/{zone_id}/custom_hostnames/{custom_hostname_id}" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer <API_TOKEN>" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "ssl": {
      "method": "txt",
      "type": "dv"
    }
  }'
```

</Details>

After sending the PATCH, share the updated DCV tokens from `ssl.validation_records` with your customer. Make sure the tokens are placed before they expire again.

:::tip
If you are managing DCV tokens on behalf of your customers, consider using [Delegated DCV](/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/security/certificate-management/issue-and-validate/validate-certificates/delegated-dcv/) to avoid manual token delivery altogether.
:::

## SSL.com: separate DCV cycles for RSA and ECDSA

When using [SSL.com](/ssl/reference/certificate-authorities/) as the certificate authority, Cloudflare requests both an RSA certificate and an ECDSA certificate. Each certificate type requires its own DCV cycle with a separate set of tokens.

Your customer must place both sets of tokens before either certificate can issue. The `ssl.validation_records` field in the API response returns one entry per certificate type — confirm that records for both are placed in DNS.
