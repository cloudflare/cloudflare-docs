Before configuring session durations, consider your organization's security requirements and user experience priorities. Different organizations have different needs based on their risk tolerance, compliance requirements, and user workflows.

## High-security scenarios (more frequent reauthentication)

**When to use:**

- Access to highly sensitive data (financial records, customer PII, production databases).
- Compliance requirements (HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOC 2) mandate regular verification.
- Shared or public devices, contractor or temporary access.
- Post-security incident heightened security posture.

**Typical configuration:** Session durations of 1-8 hours with application-specific overrides for critical systems.

**Trade-offs:** Reduces credential exposure window and ensures active user presence, but increases user interruption and potential productivity impact.

## Balanced security scenarios (recommended for most)

**When to use:**

- Standard business applications with moderate sensitivity.
- Mix of full-time employees and contractors.
- Typical remote workforce with trusted corporate devices.

**Typical configuration:** Session durations of 24 hours to 7 days with silent reauthentication enabled.

**Trade-offs:** Daily or weekly verification maintains security with minimal user disruption, reasonable balance for most organizations.

## Low-friction scenarios (less frequent reauthentication)

**When to use:**

- Low-sensitivity internal tools.
- Highly trusted employee base with strong device posture.
- Strong compensating controls (device certificates, continuous posture checks).

**Typical configuration:** Session durations of 7-30 days, may enable Device Authentication Identity.

**Trade-offs:** Minimal user interruption and maximum productivity, but requires trust in device security posture and may not meet strict compliance requirements.

## Three core concepts: Cloudflare One Client tunnel, Cloudflare One Client session, and Access sessions

It is important to understand three conceptually different things that are often confused:

1. **Cloudflare One Client tunnel** — A persistent device registration with no expiry. The underlying WireGuard or MASQUE tunnel remains connected.
2. **Cloudflare One Client session** — Controls how often a user must reauthenticate to use Cloudflare One Client for Gateway policies.
3. **Access sessions** — Controls how long a user can access Access applications.

<Aside type="note">
	The Cloudflare One Client tunnel itself is **not interrupted** when sessions
	expire. When a session expires, the tunnel remains connected but traffic is
	blocked until the user reauthenticates. This is different from traditional
	VPNs where the tunnel disconnects on session expiry.
</Aside>
