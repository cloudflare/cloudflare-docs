Cloudflare Tunnel creates a secure, outbound-only link between your infrastructure and Cloudflare's network, and gives that link a stable identity inside your Cloudflare account.

Because a tunnel's connections open from within your network and stay held open to Cloudflare, the resources behind it need no public IP address and no inbound ports left open. Traffic reaches those resources over the tunnel instead of across the public Internet.

## The connection starts with `cloudflared`

_Reach Cloudflare by connecting outward, so your resources stay private._

`cloudflared` is the daemon that opens and maintains a tunnel's connections to Cloudflare. It dials out from inside your network, which lets requests and responses move in both directions without your origin ever exposing a public address.

That inversion is the heart of how Tunnel works: instead of Cloudflare reaching in to a public address on your server, `cloudflared` reaches out to Cloudflare first, and Cloudflare then routes traffic back down those already-open connections to your resources.

<TunnelOutbound client:visible>

`cloudflared` opens connections outward from a private network to Cloudflare, and traffic reaches your resources back through them. A request sent before the tunnel exists is turned away at the network boundary, because nothing inside is listening for inbound connections.

</TunnelOutbound>

For more information, refer to [Configuration](/tunnel/configuration/).

## Tunnel identity and connectors

_Separate the tunnel you configure from the software that runs it._

Creating a tunnel assigns it a unique ID — the `#123` shown in the figure below, for example. Routes and other Cloudflare services refer to the tunnel by this ID.

A connector is a single running instance of `cloudflared`, and each one holds several connections open to Cloudflare. Because every connector reports the same tunnel ID, you can add connectors to scale a tunnel without touching its routes. Additional connectors are called replicas and usually run on separate hosts, so if one connector drops out, Cloudflare can route new traffic through another.

<TunnelIdentity client:visible>

Two `cloudflared` connectors stand behind a single, stable tunnel identity, so traffic keeps flowing when one of them stops. Stop connector B and every new request travels through connector A, while the tunnel ID and its routes stay unchanged.

</TunnelIdentity>

For more information, refer to [Replicas and high availability](/tunnel/configuration/#replicas-and-high-availability) and [Load balancing](/tunnel/concepts/routing/#load-balancing).

## How traffic reaches a resource

_Route traffic to your resources through the tunnel._

An open tunnel does not, on its own, make any resource reachable. What reaches a resource is decided by routing configuration: which traffic should travel over the tunnel, and which resource each request is bound for.

- **Published applications** map public hostnames (for example, `app.example.com`) to services sitting behind the tunnel.
- **Private network routes** send traffic for private hostnames, IP addresses, or network ranges through the tunnel.
- **Workers VPC** gives Workers a path to private services through the tunnel.

A single tunnel can serve many resources at once, and those resources can use different kinds of routing. Whenever traffic matches the routing configuration, Cloudflare hands it to an available tunnel connection; `cloudflared` picks it up on the other side and delivers it to a resource it can reach inside your network.

Connectivity and access are separate concerns: the tunnel carries traffic, while access to each resource is governed on its own.

<TunnelTraffic client:visible>

Routing configuration hands traffic to an available tunnel connection and on to `cloudflared`, which delivers it to a resource it can reach. A request with no matching route fails at once, and a routed request with no tunnel fails at Cloudflare's edge, because both the route and the tunnel have to be in place.

</TunnelTraffic>

For more information, refer to [Routing](/tunnel/concepts/routing/), [Private networks](/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/private-net/), and [Workers VPC](/workers-vpc/configuration/tunnel/).
